# Risk Report Skill

## When to use
- A risk register needs to be summarised for the board or audit committee
- A new initiative or investment requires a risk assessment section
- A business unit needs to report its top risks to management
- A project is stalled and the risks driving the delay need to be documented
- Regulatory or compliance risks need to be communicated to a non-technical audience

## Risk assessment framework

### Risk scoring
Rate each risk on two dimensions:
- **Likelihood**: 1 (rare) → 5 (almost certain)
- **Impact**: 1 (negligible) → 5 (catastrophic)
- **Risk score**: Likelihood × Impact (1–25)
  - 1–6: Low
  - 7–12: Moderate
  - 13–19: High
  - 20–25: Critical

### Risk report structure
1. **Risk summary** — total number of risks by tier (Critical / High / Moderate / Low). Flag any new risks since the last report or risks that have changed tier.
2. **Critical and high risks** — for each: risk name, description, likelihood, impact, score, owner, current controls, residual risk, and recommended action.
3. **Emerging risks** — risks that are not yet rated high but are trending upward. Flag the early warning signals.
4. **Risk appetite statement** — is the current risk profile within the organisation's stated risk appetite? If not, flag the gap.
5. **Mitigations in progress** — status of active risk mitigation actions. Are they on track?
6. **Decision required** — any risks that require board or management approval to accept, transfer, or mitigate.

### Individual risk entry format
```
Risk: [Name]
Category: [Financial | Operational | Strategic | Regulatory | Reputational | Technology]
Description: [What could happen and why]
Likelihood: [1–5] | Impact: [1–5] | Score: [1–25] | Tier: [Low/Moderate/High/Critical]
Owner: [Role or team responsible]
Current controls: [What is already in place]
Residual risk: [Risk level after controls]
Recommended action: [Specific action, owner, and deadline]
```

## Writing rules for risk reports
- Write risk descriptions as events, not conditions. ("The enterprise client terminates the contract early" — not "contract risk".)
- Always state the financial or operational impact in quantified terms where possible.
- Distinguish inherent risk (before controls) from residual risk (after controls).
- Avoid vague mitigations ("monitor the situation", "improve controls"). Each mitigation must name what, who, and by when.
- Flag risks that have no current mitigation plan explicitly.

## Tone
Objective, precise, and direct. Risk reports are read by audit committees and boards — avoid hedging language and be clear about what is uncertain.
